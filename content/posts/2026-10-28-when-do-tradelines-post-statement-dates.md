> **Short answer:** A tradeline posts after the card's statement closing date, when the issuer sends its monthly report to the bureaus. Industry guides put the total wait at roughly 15 to 45 days from being added. Our own listings state posting within 1-15 business days after the statement date shown, and recommend ordering at least 3 business days before that date to make the current cycle.

## Why the statement date is the date that matters

Credit card issuers send account data to the bureaus on a monthly cycle, typically on or shortly after the statement closing date. The report is a snapshot of the account at that moment. The due date, usually 21 to 25 days later, is a different date and does not drive reporting.

When someone is added as an authorized user, the issuer processes the addition internally, but it is normally queued for the next scheduled data transmission rather than sent immediately.

## A worked timeline

Imagine a card whose statement closes on the **22nd**:

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| At least 3 business days before the 22nd | Recommended order cut-off. Your information is sent to the account's issuer team to add you as an authorized user |
| The 22nd | Statement closes and the account's data is captured for reporting |
| Within 1-15 business days after the 22nd | Issuer reports, bureaus process, and the account typically appears on your report |

If you are added after the 22nd, the account usually waits for the following month's statement and posts one cycle later.

## Why it is not the same on every report

- **Issuer choices.** Issuers decide whether to report authorized users and to which bureaus. Experian notes they are not required to report.
- **Bureau lag.** Each bureau ingests data on its own schedule, so one report can update days before another.
- **Batching.** Some issuers process authorized-user additions in batches, which can push you into a later cycle.
- **Weekends and holidays.** Posting windows count business days, so bank holidays extend them.

## What can stop a tradeline from posting

1. **An active fraud alert or credit freeze.** Lift it before the account is reported. Our Terms of Service make this the buyer's responsibility.
2. **A negative account with the same bank.** If you have a derogatory account with the same issuer, the line may not post.
3. **Mismatched identity details.** Name, address and Social Security number must match your credit file.
4. **An account closed before the report.** The account must stay open through the reporting cycle.

## How to check whether it posted

1. Wait until the window after the statement date has passed.
2. Pull all three bureau reports. Free apps often show only one or two bureaus, so a "missing" account may simply be on the other report.
3. Look for the creditor name, the date opened, the limit and your status as authorized user.
4. If it is missing after the window, contact support by email with a three-bureau report, as the Terms of Service require for any warranty claim.

## Planning around a deadline

Work backward from the date you need the improved profile. Allow the order cut-off, the statement date, the posting window and a buffer. Industry guides recommend starting 45 to 90 days before an application, and mortgage applicants should start earlier still because lenders may not weigh authorized-user accounts the same way. ## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a tradeline take to show on my credit report?
Guides range from about 15 to 45 days from the date you are added. Our listings state posting within 1-15 business days after the statement date.

### What is the cut-off for making this month's statement?
We recommend ordering at least 3 business days before the statement date. Orders placed within 1-2 business days of the date, or after it, move to the next cycle.

### Will it post to all three bureaus?
Most lines report to all three, but this cannot be guaranteed because it depends on the issuer and your individual credit file.

### Can I speed it up?
Not once you are added. You can avoid delays by lifting freezes and alerts, verifying your information and ordering before the cut-off.

### Does Credit Karma show everything?
Free apps may show only some bureaus. Use a three-bureau report to verify.
