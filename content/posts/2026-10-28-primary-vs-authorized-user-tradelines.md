> **Short answer:** A primary tradeline is an account you own and are legally responsible for, so it builds your own credit history. An authorized-user (AU) tradeline belongs to someone else and lends you its history. Both can appear on your report, but newer FICO versions give AU accounts less weight than your own accounts, and some underwriters ignore them. The strongest file uses AU lines as a boost and primary accounts as the foundation.

## The core differences

| Factor | Primary | Authorized user |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | You open and control it | Someone else controls it |
| Legal liability | You | Primary cardholder (myFICO states the AU is not legally responsible) |
| Hard inquiry to open | Yes | No |
| Reports on your file | Normally, to the bureaus the lender uses | Only if the issuer reports AUs |
| Score weight | Full | Often lower in recent FICO versions |
| If the owner misses a payment | Not applicable | It can appear on your file; you can ask to be removed |
| Underwriting value | Full | Limited for some manual mortgage reviews |

## How scoring models treat each

**FICO.** myFICO says authorized-user accounts can appear on a credit report and affect the score, positively or negatively. In older FICO versions they are treated like the primary account holder's. In recent versions they have less impact than primary accounts.

**Trended data.** A third-party analysis of FICO 10T notes that, because the model reads balance history over time, a card where the owner carries high balances month after month may help less than one paid down regularly.

**Utilization still counts.** An AU card reporting a $500 balance on a $10,000 limit shows 5% utilization. The same card at $9,000 shows 90%. Ask how the owner uses the card before you rely on it.

## When an authorized-user tradeline makes sense

- **Thin or new files.** A seasoned account can add years of age and a larger total limit quickly.
- **Utilization pressure.** Extra available credit can lower overall utilization if the balance on the AU account is low.
- **A timed goal.** If you have a card or loan application coming, an AU account can post faster than a new account you open yourself, since opening one means a hard inquiry and a new, young account.

## When a primary account is the better move

- You need an account you control that keeps reporting after any AU arrangement ends.
- You are preparing for a **manually underwritten mortgage**. Fannie Mae's guide says authorized-user tradelines generally cannot be considered in manual underwriting unless another borrower owns the account or you can document 12 months as the sole payer.
- You want history that survives a change in the owner's habits.

## The best approach: use both

1. Open and keep at least one primary card, even a low-limit one, and use it lightly.
2. Pay in full before the statement closes so the reported balance stays low.
3. Add AU history where it fits a timeline, and keep it only while its history is positive.
4. Check all three reports a few weeks later to confirm what posted and where.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an authorized user be held responsible for the debt?
According to myFICO, an authorized user is not legally responsible for the account's debt. The primary cardholder is.

### What happens if the primary cardholder pays late?
The late payment can show on the AU's report as well. myFICO says an AU can request to be removed from a delinquent account, and the account will then be removed from their credit report.

### Does an authorized-user account count the same as my own?
Not in recent FICO versions, where AU accounts carry less weight, and not for some mortgage underwriting rules.

### Does the AU account help my credit age?
If it reports, the account's open date can influence the age statistics on your report. How much that matters depends on the scoring model and the rest of your file.

### Will I get a physical card?
Often not. In marketplace tradeline purchases, you typically do not get card access. The value is the reporting history only.
